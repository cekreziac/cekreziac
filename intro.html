// 01 — DEFINITION

What is Cyber Security?

root@cybersec:~$ define cybersecurity
▶ The practice of protecting systems, networks, programs,
  and data from digital attacks, unauthorized access, and damage.
🛡️
CORE DEFINITION

Cybersecurity is the discipline of protecting computers, servers, mobile devices, electronic systems, networks, and data from malicious attacks. It is also known as information technology security or electronic information security. The field encompasses everything from securing operating systems to training users on best practices.

🌐 NETWORK SECURITY

Protecting computer networks from intruders, whether targeted attackers or opportunistic malware.

📱 APPLICATION SECURITY

Keeping software and devices free from threats. A compromised application could provide access to the data it's designed to protect.

💾 INFORMATION SECURITY

Protecting the integrity and privacy of data, both in storage and in transit.

⚙️ OPERATIONAL SECURITY

Processes and decisions for handling and protecting data assets, including user permissions and data storage procedures.

☁️ CLOUD SECURITY

Securing data, applications, and infrastructure hosted in cloud environments using policies, controls, and technologies.

🔄 DISASTER RECOVERY

How an organization responds to and recovers from a cyber-security incident or any other event that causes loss of operations or data.

// 02 — TIMELINE

History of Cyber Security

1960s — THE BEGINNING
The concept of cybersecurity was born alongside the internet's predecessor, ARPANET. Early computer scientists began noticing that systems could be compromised. The term "hacker" originally referred to curious programmers who explored system limits.
1971 — FIRST COMPUTER WORM
The Creeper program, created by Bob Thomas at BBN Technologies, became the first self-replicating program. It spread across ARPANET displaying "I'M THE CREEPER, CATCH ME IF YOU CAN!" — prompting the creation of Reaper, the first antivirus program.
1983 — THE WORD "VIRUS"
Fred Cohen formally defined the term computer virus in his dissertation. The same year, the movie WarGames depicted a teenager hacking into NORAD, bringing cybersecurity to public consciousness for the first time.
1988 — MORRIS WORM
Robert Tappan Morris released the Morris Worm, the first worm to gain widespread attention. It crashed ~6,000 computers (10% of the internet at the time). Morris became the first person convicted under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.
1990s — ANTIVIRUS ERA
As personal computers became common, so did viruses. Companies like Symantec, McAfee, and Norton emerged. The first SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) protocol was released by Netscape in 1995, enabling secure online transactions.
2000 — ILOVEYOU VIRUS
The ILOVEYOU worm spread via email and infected over 50 million computers worldwide in just 10 days, causing an estimated $10 billion in damage. It highlighted the vulnerability of social engineering attacks.
2003 — ANONYMOUS & HACKTIVISM
The hacktivist group Anonymous emerged from 4chan. Hacktivism — hacking for political or social causes — became a recognized movement. Cybersecurity evolved beyond corporate concerns into geopolitical territory.
2010 — STUXNET: CYBERWARFARE
Stuxnet, a joint US-Israeli cyberweapon, physically destroyed Iranian nuclear centrifuges. It was the first malware designed to cause real-world, physical damage — marking the dawn of state-sponsored cyber warfare.
2013 — SNOWDEN REVELATIONS
Edward Snowden leaked classified NSA documents revealing mass surveillance programs. This sparked a global debate on privacy, encryption, and government overreach, accelerating adoption of end-to-end encryption.
2017 — WANNACRY RANSOMWARE
The WannaCry ransomware attack hit 200,000+ computers across 150 countries, shutting down hospitals, banks, and telecom companies. It exploited a vulnerability in Windows called EternalBlue, allegedly stolen from the NSA.
2020s — AI & ZERO-DAYS
Cybersecurity entered the AI era. Attackers now use AI-generated phishing, deepfakes, and automated exploit tools. Defenders use machine learning for anomaly detection. The cybersecurity arms race has never moved faster.
// 03 — CORE PRINCIPLES

The CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is the foundational model of information security. Every security policy, tool, and strategy can be traced back to protecting one or more of these three principles.

C
CONFIDENTIALITY
Ensuring information is accessible only to those authorized to have access. Achieved through encryption, access controls, and authentication.
I
INTEGRITY
Safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of information. Data must not be altered by unauthorized parties. Ensured through hashing, checksums, and digital signatures.
A
AVAILABILITY
Ensuring authorized users have access to information and resources when needed. Protected against DoS attacks, hardware failures, and outages.
// 04 — CLASSIFICATION

Cyber Security Teams

Security teams are organized by their role — offensive, defensive, or collaborative. Understanding each team's purpose is key to building a robust security posture.

OFFENSIVE
🔴 Red Team
The Red Team acts as the attacker. They simulate real-world adversaries by conducting penetration tests, exploiting vulnerabilities, and attempting to breach defenses — all with permission. Their goal is to find weaknesses before real attackers do.
> pentest / exploit / social engineering / physical intrusion
DEFENSIVE
🔵 Blue Team
The Blue Team defends. They monitor networks, analyze logs, respond to incidents, patch vulnerabilities, and implement security controls. They operate Security Operations Centers (SOCs) and respond to alerts in real time.
> SIEM / threat hunting / incident response / hardening
COLLABORATIVE
🟣 Purple Team
The Purple Team bridges Red and Blue. They facilitate collaboration between attackers and defenders to maximize the effectiveness of both. By sharing findings in real-time, they accelerate improvement of defenses and attack simulations.
> knowledge transfer / joint exercises / feedback loops
INTELLIGENCE
🟡 Yellow Team
The Yellow Team consists of builders — developers and engineers who create secure systems from the ground up. They focus on DevSecOps, integrating security into the software development lifecycle (SDLC) from the start.
> DevSecOps / secure coding / code review / SAST/DAST
INTELLIGENCE
🟢 Green Team
The Green Team focuses on improving Blue Team capabilities based on Red Team findings. They automate security processes, improve detection rules, and build security tools that make defenses smarter and faster.
> automation / detection engineering / tooling
EDUCATION
🟠 Orange Team
The Orange Team focuses on security awareness and education. They train employees, run phishing simulations, and build a security-conscious culture within the organization — because the human is often the weakest link.
> awareness training / phishing simulations / culture
// 05 — CLASSIFICATION

Types of Hackers

🎩
WHITE HAT
Ethical hackers who work with organizations to find and fix vulnerabilities. They operate with full permission and follow legal boundaries. Often certified (CEH, OSCP).
🎩
BLACK HAT
Malicious hackers who break into systems illegally for personal gain, financial profit, espionage, or simply to cause damage. Their actions are criminal offenses worldwide.
🪖
GREY HAT
Operate between ethical and unethical. They may hack without permission but disclose vulnerabilities to the owner — sometimes requesting a fee. Neither fully legal nor malicious.
🟢
GREEN HAT
Beginner or "newbie" hackers who are learning. They are eager to grow their skills but may accidentally cause damage. Often found in hacking forums and CTF competitions.
✊
HACKTIVIST
Hack for political, social, or ideological causes. Groups like Anonymous use DDoS attacks and data leaks to protest governments or corporations. Motivation is activism, not profit.
🏛️
STATE-SPONSORED
Government-backed hackers conducting espionage, sabotage, or intelligence gathering against foreign nations. Examples: APT28 (Russia), Lazarus Group (North Korea), APT41 (China).
💣
CYBERTERRORIST
Use cyberattacks to cause fear, disruption, or casualties — targeting critical infrastructure like power grids, water systems, and hospitals to achieve political or ideological goals.
🤖
SCRIPT KIDDIE
Inexperienced hackers who use pre-written tools and scripts without understanding how they work. They lack technical depth but can still cause real damage using existing exploit kits.
🕵️
INSIDER THREAT
Employees, contractors, or business partners who misuse authorized access to harm the organization — either maliciously or through negligence. Among the hardest threats to detect.
// 06 — CLASSIFICATION

Penetration Testing Methods

⬜
WHITE BOX TESTING
The tester has full knowledge of the system — source code, architecture, credentials, and internal documentation. This allows the most thorough and efficient testing. Also called Glass Box or Clear Box testing. Best for deep vulnerability assessments.
Knowledge: FULL  |  Speed: FAST  |  Depth: MAXIMUM
⬛
BLACK BOX TESTING
The tester has zero prior knowledge — simulating an external attacker with no insider information. They must discover everything from scratch: IP ranges, services, vulnerabilities. This is the closest to a real-world attack scenario.
Knowledge: NONE  |  Speed: SLOW  |  Realism: MAXIMUM
🔲
GREY BOX TESTING
A hybrid approach where the tester has partial knowledge — typically user-level credentials or some internal documentation, but not full access. Balances realism with efficiency. Most common in real-world engagements.
Knowledge: PARTIAL  |  Speed: MODERATE  |  Balance: HIGH
🔍
VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT
A systematic review of security weaknesses in an information system. Unlike penetration testing, it identifies and classifies vulnerabilities without exploiting them. Uses automated scanners like Nessus, OpenVAS, or Qualys.
Goal: IDENTIFY  |  Exploit: NO  |  Automation: HIGH
🎯
TARGETED TESTING
Both the tester and IT team work together with full visibility. Sometimes called the "lights-on" approach. Useful for training blue teams and testing detection capabilities. Less realistic but highly educational.
Collaboration: FULL  |  Realism: LOW  |  Training: HIGH
🕶️
BLIND TESTING
The tester receives minimal information (just the company name), while the security team knows an attack is coming. Tests both attack and defense capabilities simultaneously. Provides insight into how quickly teams detect intrusions.
Attacker info: MIN  |  Defender aware: YES  |  Detection test: HIGH
🌐
NETWORK PENETRATION TESTING
Focuses on network infrastructure — routers, switches, firewalls, and services. Testers look for open ports, misconfigured services, unpatched systems, and weak protocols (Telnet, FTP, SNMPv1).
Target: NETWORK  |  Tools: Nmap, Metasploit, Wireshark
🔗
SOCIAL ENGINEERING
Attacks targeting the human element. Testers use phishing emails, pretexting, vishing (voice phishing), and baiting to manipulate employees into revealing credentials or granting access — often bypassing all technical defenses.
Target: HUMANS  |  Technical skill: LOW  |  Effectiveness: VERY HIGH
// 07 — THREAT LANDSCAPE

Common Cyber Threats

🐟
PHISHING
Fraudulent emails or messages that trick users into revealing credentials or installing malware. Most common initial attack vector.
🔒
RANSOMWARE
Encrypts victim's files and demands payment for the decryption key. Can bring entire organizations to a halt.
🪱
MALWARE
Malicious software including viruses, worms, trojans, spyware, and adware designed to damage or gain unauthorized access.
💥
DDoS ATTACK
Distributed Denial of Service — floods a target with traffic from thousands of infected machines, making services unavailable.
🎭
MAN-IN-THE-MIDDLE
Attacker secretly intercepts and relays communications between two parties — stealing data, credentials, or session tokens.
💉
SQL INJECTION
Malicious SQL code is injected into queries, allowing attackers to view, modify, or delete database contents without authorization.
🌐
XSS ATTACK
Cross-Site Scripting injects malicious scripts into trusted websites, stealing cookies, session tokens, or redirecting users.
0️⃣
ZERO-DAY EXPLOIT
Attacks exploiting a previously unknown vulnerability before the vendor has had time to create and release a patch.
🔑
CREDENTIAL STUFFING
Using leaked username/password combinations from data breaches to gain unauthorized access to other accounts.
🎣
SPEAR PHISHING
Highly targeted phishing attacks aimed at specific individuals using personalized information to increase success rates.

🎓 HOW TO START IN CYBER SECURITY

Begin with foundational IT knowledge (CompTIA A+, Network+), then move to Security+. Practice on platforms like TryHackMe, HackTheBox, and PicoCTF. Learn Linux, Python, and networking deeply. Build a home lab. Participate in CTF competitions. The field rewards curiosity, persistence, and continuous learning — there's no single path.
